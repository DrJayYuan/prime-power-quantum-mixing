Exact uniform mixing on odd-prime-power quantum paths

A single random readout time can erase all dependence of the final site probabilities on the starting site of a uniform quantum chain. We prove this for paths with pa − 1 sites, for every odd prime p and every integer a ≥ 2. Relations among the mode energies divide them into blocks. Two elementary sampling laws on those blocks produce the required quantum coherences with strict slack. The resulting readout law can be supported on finitely many nonnegative integer times. For eight sites we give a certified seventeen-time schedule.

Model and theorem

Let A be the adjacency matrix of the unweighted path on N sites. Set ℏ and the hopping amplitude to one. Write U(t) = exp(itA) and M(t)uv = |U(t)uv|². Reversing the sign of the Hamiltonian gives the same probabilities. Each preparation has one site measurement, at a time sampled independently of the initial site. Write J for the all-ones matrix.

Theorem. For q = pa, p an odd prime and a ≥ 2, put N = q − 1. There exist nonnegative integers tj and positive weights wj summing to one such that ∑j wjM(tj) = J/N. At most N²/4 + 1 times suffice.

Baptista, Coutinho and Marques constructed readout laws for three and four sites [1]. Kang gave an obstruction for eleven sites [2], then positive results for q prime, twice prime, or a power of two, and negative results for several divisibility classes [3]. Composite odd prime powers fall outside those positive and negative families. Our proof uses the moment target and convex-geometric approach of [3]. Its additional ingredients are the prime-power relation lattice and a probability law respecting that lattice.

The moment target

Write θk = 2 cos(πk/q), 1 ≤ k ≤ N, and let Ek be the spectral projectors. It suffices to realize the following off-diagonal cosine moments for all distinct k, l [3].

𝔼 cos((θk − θl)t) = −1/N for equal parity,   0 for opposite parity.

Indeed, with ∘ denoting entrywise multiplication and R the site reflection, the sine eigenvectors give D = ∑k Ek∘Ek = (2J + I + R)/(2q). The sum of Ek∘El over ordered equal-parity pairs including the diagonal is (I + R)/2, since ∑k(−1)kEk = −R. Substitution gives (q/N)D − (I + R)/(2N) = J/N.

1. The allowed phases form disjoint blocks

Let s = q/p, m = (q − 1)/2 and ζ = exp(2πi/q). Keep the even modes αj = θ2j = ζj + ζ−j for 1 ≤ j ≤ m. The odd modes are their negatives because θq−2j = −αj. An index j represents the pair of residues {j,−j} modulo q.

For each pair of nonzero residues {r,−r} modulo s, put the indices reducing to that pair in a full block Br. There are (s − 1)/2 such blocks, each of size p. The remaining (p − 1)/2 indices divisible by s form one short block. There are K = (s + 1)/2 blocks in all.

Relation lemma. All integer relations among the αj are integer linear combinations of ∑j∈B αj = 0, one for each full block B.

Proof. Encode a relation with coefficients cj by the polynomial P(X) = ∑j cj(Xj + Xq−j). It vanishes at ζ exactly when it is divisible over the rationals by the cyclotomic polynomial

Φq(X) = 1 + Xs + ⋯ + X(p−1)s.

Since deg P < q, the quotient has degree less than s. Its shifted copies occupy disjoint coefficient ranges. The coefficient of P is therefore constant on each residue class modulo s. The class of zero has coefficient zero because P has no constant term. Symmetry identifies the two classes r and −r. Conversely, a full-block sum vanishes by the same divisibility. The block indicators have disjoint support and integer coefficients, proving the claim.

By Kronecker's approximation theorem, the closure of the phases zj = exp(itαj) is precisely the compact subgroup T cut out by ∏j∈B zj = 1 on each full block. Short-block coordinates are free. Nonnegative integer times already have the same closure. Indeed, their annihilating characters satisfy ∑j cjαj ∈ 2πℤ. The left side is algebraic, so transcendence of π forces it to be zero.

2. Two sampling laws on the allowed phases

Put ω = exp(2πi/p). In the first law, sample entries of each block without replacement from the p powers of ω, in uniformly random order, independently across blocks. A full block has product one because p is odd. Every coordinate has mean zero. For distinct coordinates, 𝔼[zj zk] is zero across blocks and −1/(p − 1) within a block. The latter follows by summing all ordered pairs of different roots, whose total is −p.

3. Strict slack in the pair correlations

In the second law, assign a single phase to each block and copy it to every coordinate in that block. We require a common cross-block correlation −h with h > 1/(s − 1). Within each block the correlation is then one.

For s > 3, make a multiset of K roots using ⌊K/p⌋ full copies of all p roots and the sequence 1, ω², ω⁴, …, ωp−1. The remainder has (p + 1)/2 entries, as required by 2K = s + 1. Its sum S has modulus 1/(2 cos(π/p)) ≤ 1 by the geometric series formula. Assign the multiset to the K blocks by a uniform random permutation. Each assigned phase is a pth root, so all full-block product constraints hold. Summing over two different slots gives

−h = (|S|² − K)/[K(K − 1)],   h ≥ 1/K > 1/(s − 1).

For s = 3 we have q = 9. Set the phase on the full block to 1 and on the unconstrained singleton to −1. This gives h = 1 > 1/2.

Choose the second law with probability λ = 1/[p + (p − 1)h] and the first otherwise. Every distinct pair of even-mode coordinates now has the same correlation −c. Within a block it is λ − (1 − λ)/(p − 1), and between blocks it is −λh. Hence

c = h/[p + (p − 1)h] > 1/(ps − 1) = 1/N.

Multiply every coordinate by the same independent uniform pth root. This preserves T and all conjugate-pair correlations, and makes 𝔼[zjzk] zero, including j = k, since 2 is nonzero modulo p. These are precisely the opposite-parity moments after the negative modes are restored. Finally mix this law with normalized Haar measure on T, using the constructed law with probability 1/(Nc). Haar annihilates every nonzero energy-gap character because the energies are distinct. The mixture has the target moments and a strictly positive Haar component.

4. From phases to exact readout times

Let F map a phase tuple to the vector of off-diagonal cosine moments, keeping one pair from each reflection class (k,l) ∼ (q−l,q−k). The mixture has full support, so its barycenter lies in the relative interior of conv F(T). Indeed, a supporting functional attaining its maximum at this average must attain it everywhere by continuity. Integer-time vectors are dense in F(T). Their convex hull contains that relative interior: surround the target by a small simplex inside the relative interior, then approximate its vertices by convex combinations of orbit points. Sufficiently small perturbations preserve containment. Carathéodory's theorem gives at most m² + 1 times since there are m² reflection classes (pairs with k < l and k + l ≤ q). This proves the theorem.

Let B have one column per time, an all-ones first row and sixteen rows cos((θk − θl)t) for k < l, k + l ≤ 9. Let b have first entry one, then −1/8 for equal parities and zero otherwise. The exact weights are w = B−1b. The companion program encloses all trigonometric entries with interval arithmetic. It freezes an approximate inverse C and weight vector v as dyadic rationals and checks the induced infinity-norm bounds

‖I − CB‖∞ < 1.3 × 10−11,   ‖w − v‖∞ < 4.5 × 10−13.

The first bound implies invertibility. The second follows by dividing ‖C(b − Bv)‖∞ by 1 − ‖I − CB‖∞. Every exact weight exceeds 0.02022. A separate floating-point calculation diagonalizes the site adjacency matrix and checks all 64 transition probabilities with the dyadic weights. The maximum deviation from 1/8 was 3.81 × 10−13. Run python check_p8.py with NumPy and mpmath.

The support bound controls the number of distinct readout settings. The general proof supplies no upper bound on the largest time. The example gives a finite schedule in the chosen hopping units. It concerns ensemble readout statistics for site preparations; individual runs still produce one site outcome.
